## Reading 1: Christensen, "Know Your Customers' Jobs to Be Done"

The one idea: customers do not buy products, they hire them to make progress
on a job in a specific circumstance, and they fire them when something does
the job better. A job has functional, emotional, and social dimensions.

The milkshake story, in plain words:
- A fast food chain asked customers how to improve its milkshakes and got
  nowhere. Researchers instead watched when shakes were bought and asked what
  job the buyer was hiring the shake to do.
- Morning commuters hired it to survive a long, boring drive: thick, slow to
  drink, one-handed, no crumbs, filling until lunch. Its real competition was
  bananas, bagels, and coffee, not other milkshakes.
- Afternoon parents hired the same product for a different job: a treat that
  lets them say yes to their kids and feel like good parents.
- Fixes followed the job, not the recipe: thicker shakes and faster pickup
  for commuters.

Say it in class this way: the job, not the product category or the customer's
demographics, defines the competition and the design brief.

Sources: https://online.hbs.edu/blog/post/jobs-to-be-done-examples
and https://dovetail.com/research/jobs-to-be-done/

## Reading 2: Horowitz, "Good Product Manager / Bad Product Manager"

The one idea: a good PM is the CEO of the product, meaning responsibility
without authority. They measure themselves by the product's success and make
no excuses about funding, headcount, or direction.

Contrasts worth being able to say out loud:
- Good PMs know the market, the product, and the competition cold, and define
  the target and the what; bad PMs wait to be told what to do.
- Good PMs take written positions on hard calls; bad PMs voice opinions
  verbally and blame the powers that be afterward.
- Good PMs anticipate serious flaws and build real solutions; bad PMs put
  out fires all day and become gofers for other teams.
- Good PMs decompose problems; bad PMs merge every problem into one big one
  and obsess over matching competitor features instead of customers and revenue.

Original essay: https://a16z.com/good-product-manager-bad-product-manager/
(as listed in the public notes at https://www.pelayoarbues.com/literature-notes/Articles/Good-Product-ManagerBad-Product-Manager)
