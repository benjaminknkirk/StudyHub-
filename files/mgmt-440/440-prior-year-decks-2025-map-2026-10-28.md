## UCLA_L2_Oct_8_2025.pdf → mainly 2026 Class 2 (Thu Nov 5: PMF, Competitive Landscape & Differentiation)
- Recap of L1: PM role, innovation, JTBD, Product Critique discussion
- Agenda sources: "Not All Good Products Make Good Businesses" (Traynor), Apple Product Strategy 2011 (Jobs), Inspired (Cagan), Escaping the Build Trap Part 1 (Perri)
- Product Market Fit: good product ≠ good business; PMF = market needs satisfaction, user delight, acceptance/growth, clear differentiation, sustainable business model; "Do people want it? Would they pay for it? Could I build it?"
- Target market: big + frequent problems best; small + rare is a trap; small + frequent and big + infrequent both workable
- Pricing intro; Crossing the Chasm; competitive analysis in 5 steps (identify competitors, analyze products/services, research pricing, analyze strengths/weaknesses and customer feedback, evaluate marketing)
- Differentiation levers: packaging, features, segmentation, customer service/CX, ease of use, time to value, price; branded/differentiated = premium vs commoditized = price wars
- Product viability: effort to build, ability to recover cost, grow market without growing cost (margins, cloud/support costs)
- PRD: what it is (storybook / compass), who it serves (engineering, UX, marketing/sales), key components (business objectives/press release, market segment & personas, product description & user stories, functional requirements, technical specs & architecture, data/security, success metrics, analytics/reporting, wireframes/mocks, cost estimate & pricing, release plan/roadmap, support & maintenance, feedback)
- Wireframes (low-fi skeletal) vs mockups (high-fi static visual)
- 2025 Assignment 2: PRD Part 1 (PR, value proposition, problem statement, target segment, JTBD, pain point, market size, competitive landscape, differentiation, wireframes, functional/technical specs)
- 2025 Quiz #2 sat in this lecture; guest: Nidhi Wadmark (Visa, PayPal, IBM, Kaiser)

## UCLA_L3_Oct_15_2025.pdf → mainly 2026 Class 3 (Thu Nov 12: Data, Metrics & AI), plus value prop for Critique/Final
- Recap: JTBD & customer focus, PMF, pricing & positioning, PRD fundamentals
- Value proposition & PR: value prop as north star for messaging (Uber vs taxi, Apple iPhone "magical" experience examples); how to write one (understand customers, analyze competitors, know your product, target audience language, be unique, keep it simple)
- Product lifecycle; Innovator's Dilemma challenge
- Data-driven decision making: identify needs in ideation, prioritize features on usage metrics, track KPIs post-launch; pre-launch A/B test prototypes, analyze usage data, optimize CX
- Pitfalls: too much customer data without JTBD, correlation ≠ causation, "best for whom?" (business / customers / shareholders / employees)
- Measuring success: business vs product; Objectives & Key Results (OKRs); SaaS KPIs — churn rate, MRR, revenue churn, ARR, CMRR, CAC, CLV/LTV, Lead Velocity Rate, NPS; "Not everything that can be counted counts…"
- Journey of a product: problem → design → decide → development → launch phases
- 2025 Assignment 3: PRD continued (adds experimentation, data analytics, success metrics & KPIs aligned to company goals)
- 2025 Quiz #3 sat in this lecture; guest: Kittu Kolluri (Neotribe Ventures)

## UCLA_L4_Oct_22_2025.pdf → mainly 2026 Class 4 (Thu Nov 19: MVP, Launch & Go-to-Market)
- Recap: JTBD, PMF & differentiation, PRD, value prop, KPIs & metrics
- Agenda sources: Hooked (Eyal), The Best Story Wins, Thinking Fast and Slow (Kahneman), "MVP is not a product, it's a process" (Brikman)
- Strategy vs execution: strategy = JTBD, segmentation, competitive analysis, viable solutions, PRD & wireframes; execution = build & launch MVP, feedback, iterate, complete product, marketing & sales
- MVP: minimum features to satisfy early customers and validate the idea; must include design/usability/CX, not just function; test the market and course-correct before Crossing the Chasm; phased feature prioritization
- Development process: Waterfall (static/linear, hardware) vs Agile (iterative, software/SaaS); Scrum & sprints, fail fast; test early and often on mocks/prototypes before coding
- The 5-act interview; product positioning; launch & marketing
- Pricing & monetization: target market research, COGS/resources, profitability/margins, physical vs digital, perceived value; cost-based vs value-based (value-based for luxury, customized, differentiated, niche)
- Sales enablement: engage marketing & sales from the start of GTM planning
- 2025 Quiz #4 sat in this lecture; Final PRD & presentations spec (value prop, problem, market size/competitive, wireframes, MVP, pricing, functional/technical specs, data analytics, experimentation, success metrics & KPIs)
- Guest: Paul Joseph (construction-tech founder/CPO)

## UCLA_L5_Oct_29_2025.pdf → mainly 2026 Class 5 (Thu Dec 3: Evolution, Lifecycle & Final Presentations)
- Shortest deck (13 pp), mostly course wrap: learning objectives recap
- Journey of a product (full diagram); post-launch activities: MVP → MMP, marketing to Cross the Chasm, direct sales & channels, promotions/campaigns, incentive plans, partnerships
- Product roadmap: align stakeholders, prioritize, track progress, coordinate, consensus, transparency
- Kill vs Reboot: kill when no longer profitable, users not using it, cost outweighs benefits, compliance/privacy issues — first ask whether innovation, UX, cost cuts, or a cheap fix can save it
- Sunsetting: units/revenue, dev resources consumed, support effort, value of redeployed resources; build an end-of-life roadmap, communicate to sales/marketing/customers, reallocate freed resources
- Other considerations: ESG, security, ethical use of data, building socially responsible products, leading without authority, managing up/down/sideways, staying agile in ambiguity
- Final presentations & wrap-up
