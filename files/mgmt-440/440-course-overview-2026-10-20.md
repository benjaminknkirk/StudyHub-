## How learning and project work runs in this chat (integrated Oct 27, 2026)
Per Ben's integration task from the science learning chat, Oct 27, 2026. Canonical: ~/workspace/school/science-of-learning-2026-update.md and the 2026 update section in ~/workspace/school/daily-study-protocol.md.
- Concepts/frameworks: Ben attempts first (own judgement / framework application / case take written down), then discussion or AI feedback, then he re-states or re-applies it in his own words without looking. If he cannot reproduce it, it is not learned yet. AI never drafts first on work meant to build his judgement; he drafts, AI critiques. (Bastani et al. 2025: unguarded AI lowered later unaided performance 17%.)
- Feedback on his work is high-information only: which part, why it is weak, what a strong version does differently. No right/wrong-only or praise-only feedback, and never feedback aimed at the person instead of the work.
- Self-explanation is the active ingredient: when reviewing a case or framework, ask why a step follows, not just what the step is.
- Productive failure for concepts/transfer only (cold attempt before reading a new framework); for procedural deliverables, worked example first.
- Spaced check-ins on core frameworks at about 1d, 3-4d, 8-10d: quick closed-book recall of the framework plus one application, checked the same session.
- Error and revision log for project work: deliverable / what was weak and why / corrected principle, resurfaced before the next similar deliverable.

Organizing rules added Oct 28, 2026 (canonical: ~/workspace/school/organizing-information-2026-update.md, on top of the science of learning rules above):
- Every framework gets a one-page skeleton before details: what problem it solves, steps/components, when to use it, when NOT to use it, failure modes. Ben must be able to walk the skeleton from memory before applying it to a deliverable.
- REQUIRED comparison matrices for confusable frameworks/approaches, in Ben's words: rows = frameworks, columns = purpose, inputs needed, output produced, best use case, classic misuse/trap. (Case comparison d = 0.50.)
- Case work: sort cases/examples by underlying principle first, surface story second. Before analysing a new case, Ben states which principle/framework family it belongs to and why.
- Lecture/deck notes: sparse capture in class, restructure within 24h into skeleton + matrix + cue questions + 3-line summary, in his words. Filing decks (including the prior-year decks) is storage, not learning, and is never reported as prep progress.
- Team deliverables: each deliverable type (critique, metrics, PRD-style work) gets a template card: sections, what each section must prove, one strong example, one near miss. Ben drafts the structure; AI/peers critique it; he never receives a finished structure to fill in.
- Do not introduce Zettelkasten, PARA, Second Brain, or Cornell as evidence-based systems for 440 work; no controlled evidence supports them as systems.

Personalization rules added Oct 28, 2026 (canonical: ~/workspace/school/personalization-2026-update.md):
- The two 440 quizzes get the full calibration treatment: Ben predicts his score before each quiz (honest estimate, recorded before he sits it), postdicts after, and the debrief in calibration-and-grades.md records predicted, postdiction, actual, signed error, and the error sort. With only two quizzes, each is one data point in a pooled record, never a basis for sweeping conclusions about his product judgment.
- Framework-level calibration is per framework family and pooled across the course's retrieval work: no reallocation of framework study emphasis from a single case or single session (small samples carry margins of 25 points or more). Share changes follow the update's monthly thresholds (4-week pool, 40+ items, 15+ points of bias).
- Confidence in case work is logged: when Ben rates confidence on a framework call or case recommendation, record it. Calls at 80% confidence or higher that turn out wrong are his highest-value error log entries (they mark miscalibrated product intuition) and retest first.

Note-taking and encoding rules added Oct 28, 2026 (canonical: ~/workspace/school/note-taking-encoding-2026-update.md, folded into learning-plan.md):
- Framework introductions use the UPGRADED skeleton: each framework name carries a one-line function (what decision it informs, its input, what it produces) plus one question Ben attempts before the deep dive. Bare framework-name memorization is dropped (no evidence; names plus characteristics is the supported form, median d = 0.75). The existing skeleton content (problem, steps, when to use, when NOT to use, failure modes) stays.
- Class notes and deck work: sparse generative capture in class, restructured within 24h by transformation (reorganize onto the skeleton, compress in his words, cue questions, 3-line summary). Evidence: taking alone d = 0.22, taking plus reviewing about 0.75, verbatim recopying is the losing condition. Deck filing and neat recopying are never framed as prep.
- Case readings: one attempted question per section heading before reading (specific pre-questions g = 0.54 to 0.66), then read once, then closed-book retrieval of the answers.

Learning phases rules added Oct 28, 2026 (canonical: ~/workspace/school/learning-phases-protocol.md; grades/debriefs: ~/workspace/school/calibration-and-grades.md):
- Team work: working units of 3-4 only (5-7 fall to about zero effect); every member attempts the analysis/framework application individually BEFORE the team meeting; teach-back slots for frameworks are assigned BEFORE the studying happens (teaching g = 0.48 with expectancy, -0.02 without); a short individual retest or re-statement after team sessions, because 440 quizzes are individual and team fluency is not Ben's learning.
- Transfer practice, weekly: compare two cases or product decisions side by side, write the shared principle in one sentence, then apply it to one unseen case without notes. (Comparison is the transfer mechanism, about 3x vs separate study.)
- Input: pre-class readings and the prior-year decks are read once, with questions written first (what decision does this framework inform, where does it break), then closed-book retrieval. Filing/skimming decks is storage, not prep.
- The two 440 quizzes use the phases exam rules: confidence flags (H/M/L) on first pass, answer changes only for a nameable reason (misread, slip, later item info, new reasoning), and a debrief entry in calibration-and-grades.md after each quiz with the error sort (concept / discrimination / execution / time / misread).
- Adherence/feedback on deliverables: no streak or reward schemes for project work; feedback stays high-information and task-focused (which section, why weak, what strong looks like).
