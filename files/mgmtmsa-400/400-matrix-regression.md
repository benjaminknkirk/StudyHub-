# 400 Matrix: Regression Confusables (Ch 3 and Ch 4, Ch 4 to slide 34 only)

Sources: Ch 3 and 4 notes sheet (Oct 24), miss log.
Traps marked "miss log" are Ben's actual misses.

| Concept | What it is | Formula or skeleton from notes | Question cue that triggers it | Classic trap |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Correlation r | Unit-free. Only says direction and strength of the straight-line link between X and Y. | Bridge: b1 = r x (sY / sX). In simple regression, R squared equals r squared, so r = sqrt(R squared) with the slope's sign. | "How strong and which direction is the linear link?" | Miss log: set r equal to R squared (chose 0.04). Correct: R squared 0.04 with a positive slope gives r = +0.20. |
| Slope b1 | How much the fitted Y moves when X goes up by 1. Carries units: units of Y per unit of X. | b1 = sXY / sX squared. Also b1 = r x (sY / sX). Check from notes: r = 0.8, sY = 2, sX = 4 gives b1 = 0.4, not 0.8. | "When X rises by 1, how much does predicted Y change?" | Reading the slope as the correlation (0.8 instead of 0.4 in the check above). The sY / sX part converts the units. |
| R squared | The share of the spread in Y that the line explains. Video cards: 0.664 means performance explains about 66.4 percent of the variation in log price. | R squared = SSR / SST. Same thing: 1 minus SSE / SST. | "How much of the variation does the model explain?" | Three traps from notes: it does NOT mean the model is right that percent of the time; a high R squared does NOT promise good predictions on new data; it says nothing about causation (miss log Q3 on this point was correct). |
| SST | Total spread in Y. | SST = SSR + SSE | "Total variation in Y" | Swapping which part is explained and which is leftover. SST is the whole, it is not a part. |
| SSR | The part of the spread in Y that the line explains. | R squared = SSR / SST. Miss log worked example: 350 / 500 = 0.70 (correct). | "Variation explained by the line" | Not a recorded miss: keep apart from SSE by the cue word "explained". |
| SSE | The part of the spread left in the residuals. Also feeds s squared in Ch 4: s squared = SSE / (N minus 2). | R squared = 1 minus SSE / SST | "Variation left over, unexplained" | Miss log: chose the model with the LARGER SSE as the higher R squared. Rule: with the same SST, smaller SSE (less leftover) means higher R squared. |
| Residual e | The gap for a point already in the data: real Y minus fitted Y. Every Y splits as Y = Y-hat + e. | e = Y minus Y-hat. Worked example: RTX 3090, 7.63 minus 6.89 = plus 0.74. | "How far is this actual point from the line's guess?" | Confusing residual with the Ch 4 error term. Error is real Y minus the TRUE line and cannot be computed. Residual uses the FITTED line and can be computed. |
| Residual sign rule | Plus residual: point sits ABOVE the line, line under-predicted. Minus residual: point sits BELOW the line, line over-predicted. Anchor from miss log: actual on top gives plus. | Sign of e = Y minus Y-hat decides it. No other formula needed. | Any option worded "above / below the line" or "over / under-predicted" | Miss log: computed +0.8 correctly, then chose the option saying point below the line and line over-predicted. Compute, then match the wording to the sign, in that order. |
| Residual plot, U shape | A U-shaped residual plot means the relationship bends. | Least squares residuals average to zero and have no leftover straight-line pattern with X (notes, Fact 2). Shape reading itself: from miss log quiz, not the notes sheet. | "What does this residual plot pattern mean?" | Miss log Q2 on this was correct. Trap to avoid: treating a bent pattern as fine because residuals average to zero. Average zero does not clear a bent shape. |
| Outlier | A Y that is unusual FOR ITS X, next to points with similar X. | No formula in held notes. Rule from class in the notes: investigate first (data error, or a different kind of product?). Remove only with a reason, never just because it is far away. | "Is this point an outlier?" | Calling a point an outlier just because it is far out on X with no neighbours. Notes say that alone is not an outlier. |
| Influence and leverage | Influence needs BOTH: leverage (far out on X) AND a big residual. | No formula in held notes: not in held notes, verify if a leverage formula is needed. | "Which point is influential / would move the line most?" | Miss log: chose a normal-x point with a huge residual over a far-x point with a huge residual. Influence needs far x AND big residual together. Also from notes: one wild point can pull the least squares line, which is why this matters. |
| Log model slope reading (log Y) | When Y is log(price), the slope reads as a percent, not as dollars. | Slope 0.00753: performance up by 1, predicted log price up by 0.00753, about 0.75 percent higher price. Rule: multiply the log-Y slope by 100. Fitted line from class: log(price)-hat = 4.897 + 0.00753 x performance. At 200: 6.403, change back with exp(6.403), about $604. | "Interpret the slope" when the response is a log | Reading it as plus $0.00753 or plus $0.75. Also forgetting the change-back step: the line gives log price first, dollars need exp(). |
| Log model prediction reading | A prediction from a log-Y line comes out in log units first. | Same change-back step as above: exp(fitted log value) gives dollars. | "Predict the price" from a log(price) line | Stopping at the log number and reporting it as the price. Not a recorded miss: trap is stated in the notes ("the answer in dollars needs that change-back step"). |
