## Q1 — Residual sign (retest of Ch 3 Topic 3 miss)
A card's actual log price is 4.9. The fitted line predicts 5.4 for that card.
What is the residual, and where does the point sit?

A) +0.5, the point is above the line, the line under-predicted
B) +0.5, the point is below the line, the line over-predicted
C) -0.5, the point is below the line, the line over-predicted
D) 0, the point is on the line

## Q2 — Influence (retest of Ch 3 Topic 3 miss)
Four points in a price vs. performance scatter. Which one is most likely to be influential
(it pulls the fitted slope toward itself)?

A) A point with performance far from the mean AND a huge residual, sitting off the trend
B) A point with performance near the mean and a huge residual
C) A point with performance far from the mean but a tiny residual, right on the trend
D) A point with performance near the mean and a small residual

## Q3 — r vs R squared (retest of Ch 3 Topic 4 miss)
A fitted line has R squared = 0.36 and a negative slope. What is r, the correlation?

A) +0.36
B) -0.36
C) +0.60
D) -0.60

## Q4 — SSE and R squared (retest of Ch 3 Topic 4 miss)
Two models are fit to the same data, so both have SST = 200.
Model P has SSE = 50. Model Q has SSE = 80. Which model has the higher R squared, and what is it?

A) Model Q, because a larger SSE means more of the variation is explained
B) Model P, R squared = 0.75
C) They tie, because SST is the same
D) It cannot be told from SSE alone

## Answer key (for Ghost only, do not show Ben)

Q1: C. Residual = actual - predicted = 4.9 - 5.4 = -0.5. Negative residual means the point
is below the line, so the line over-predicted.
Q2: A. Influence needs both: far-out x (leverage) AND a huge residual (off the trend the
other points set). B has the residual but no leverage; C has leverage but follows the trend.
Q3: D. r is the square root of R squared (sqrt(0.36) = 0.60) and takes the slope's sign,
so r = -0.60.
Q4: B. R squared = 1 - SSE/SST. Model P: 1 - 50/200 = 0.75. Model Q: 1 - 80/200 = 0.60.
Smaller SSE leaves less unexplained, so higher R squared.
